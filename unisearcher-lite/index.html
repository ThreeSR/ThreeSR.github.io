TL;DR
  • We release three small multimodal search agents, UniSearcher-Lite-0.8B, 2B and 4B, fine-tuned from Qwen3.5 on our 17.5k-trajectory search dataset.
  • UniSearcher-Lite-4B scores 71.35 on MMSearch and 47.37 on BrowseComp-VL, above most published 7B to 9B search agents and several 30B-class ones.
  • UniSearcher-Lite-0.8B reaches 54.97 on MMSearch, above MMSearch-R1-7B and WebWatcher-7B.
  • We release the weights right after SFT, with no RL on top, so groups can skip the costly data collection and SFT stage and start their own RL from a policy that already knows how to search.
  • Everything runs on OpenVLHarness: the same harness, tools and prompts we trained with, so the numbers here are reproducible end to end.

Why small search agents

Multimodal search agents look at an image, decide what to search for, read pages, crop and zoom, and keep going until they can answer. Most open agents that do this well sit at 7B to 32B parameters, and training them takes a multi-node budget that many academic groups do not have.

We wanted to know how far the same recipe goes at the bottom of the size range. If a 4B model can search well, then a single GPU is enough to run experiments on tool use, reward design or new benchmarks, and the barrier to entry for this research area drops a lot.

Results

We evaluate every model three ways on MMSearch, MMSearch-Plus and BrowseComp-VL. Direct Answer is the base model with no tools. Agent Workflow is the same base model inside our harness, with no agentic training. UniSearcher Lite is the model after supervised fine-tuning on our data, in the same harness.

Training matters most where the base model cannot search

Before training, Qwen3.5-0.8B barely uses its tools. On MMSearch-Plus the median episode makes one tool call, and 85 of 311 episodes make none. Fine-tuning turns it into an agent that searches over many turns, and MMSearch rises from 30.41 to 54.97. At 2B the gain is +17.5 points on MMSearch. At 4B the untrained model is already a capable tool user on MMSearch, and fine-tuning pays off on the harder MMSearch-Plus (19.29 to 22.51) [TBD: BrowseComp-VL gain at 4B].

On the accuracy-size Pareto frontier

Plotting every published multimodal search agent by size and accuracy, the three UniSearcher Lite models define the frontier below 7B, and each step up the ladder adds accuracy. The 4B model is above most published 8B agents on both benchmarks, and above several 30B-class agents such as OpenSearch-VL-30B-A3B, MM-DeepResearch-32B and WebWatcher-32B.

Why we share SFT checkpoints

For small models, and for multimodal search in particular, the best results come from supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning. Recent multimodal search agents such as Neos, OpenSearch-VL, SimpleSearch-VL, Vision-DeepResearch, UniBrowse and REDSearcher all follow this SFT-then-RL path, and each reports gains from both stages. An untrained 0.8B or 2B model rarely calls its tools, so reinforcement learning has almost no successful search episodes to learn from. SFT teaches the basic loop of searching, reading and answering, and RL then works on top of a policy that already knows how to search. Our own ladder shows the same pattern from the other side: the stronger the backbone, the less SFT adds. On MMSearch, SFT lifts the 0.8B model by 24.6 points and the 2B model by 17.5, while the untrained 4B model already scores 73.10 inside our harness and SFT leaves it about level. A backbone at that level can go straight to RL.

The contrast with RL-only training is clear at the small end. MMSearch-R1 trains Qwen2.5-VL-7B with reinforcement learning alone and reaches 53.8 on MMSearch. UniSearcher-Lite-0.8B, with SFT alone, reaches 54.97 at about a ninth of the size.

The SFT stage is also the expensive part for most groups. It needs a large set of clean multi-turn trajectories and a training run long enough to fit them. To take that cost off the table, we release the models right after SFT, with no extra RL on top. They are meant as starting points: a base for RL with new rewards, new tools or new tasks, or a reference point for new benchmarks. We hope they become a foundation for the next generation of multimodal search agents, and that others build things on top of them that we have not thought of.

How we trained it

UniSearcher Lite uses the understanding recipe of UniSearcher [TBD: paper link], applied unchanged to three smaller Qwen3.5 backbones: one round of supervised fine-tuning on the same 17.5k trajectories, no RL. The data, filtering, training settings and the harness are described in the UniSearcher paper and in OpenVLHarness.
